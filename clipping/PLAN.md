# Clipping project: plan and research

Last updated: 2026-11-19 (GTA 6 launch day). Owner of this document: Claude.
Refreshed weekly by the "Weekly clipping plan" scheduled task (Mondays).

How to read the reliability tags: **official** = platform's own docs or
terms, **press** = reported by a news outlet, **vendor** = a tool or course
seller's blog (weak), **self-claimed** = someone's own statement with no
proof shown. Four research agents gathered this on 2026-11-19. Several
primary sites were blocked for them, so some official wording was read from

## 1. Bottom line

- **Expect tens of dollars, not hundreds, in the first month.** The typical
  clipper earns about $27 per campaign across about 3 approved clips
  (vendor analysis of public campaign data, Oct 2026).
- **Real pay is about half the advertised rate.** Campaigns list $1.00 per
  1,000 views; budget actually paid out works out to $0.52 to $0.72 per
  1,000 (vendor, Oct 2026). One scrape found 56% of 658 campaigns had paid
  nothing yet (vendor, Sep 2026).
- **Plain reposts are being throttled on all three platforms.** YouTube
  (Oct 2026), Instagram (Apr 2026) and TikTok all cut reach for re-uploads
  with no added value. Clips need visible additions: a hook, context text,
  a restructured cut.
- **The money is in what comes after campaigns.** Retainers of $500 to
  $1,500 a month (press) and direct streamer deals pay far more than
  per-view campaigns, but need a view history first.
- **Per-view campaigns are the proving ground, not the business.** The plan
  below uses them to build a track record for 6 weeks, then moves to direct
  deals.

## 2. What the research changed in the plan

| Before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Claude posts through your signed-in browser | Clips go out through an audited scheduling app, or you post from your phone | YouTube's and TikTok's terms bar automated access; browser automation risks the accounts. See section 6. |
| Captions and a vertical crop are enough | Every clip gets a hook, context text and a cut that starts on the reaction | Platforms no longer treat subtitles or borders as original content. |
| Judge campaigns by rate and minimum | Judge by minimum, **budget remaining** and review speed | Clippers report clearing the minimum and never being reviewed before the budget drained. |
| YouTube Shorts ad revenue as a later bonus | Ignore it | From Feb 1, 2027 it needs 10 million qualified Shorts views per 90 days, and reused clips don't count (official). |
| Sell clips to streamers in weeks 3 to 4 | Weeks 7 to 9, after about 30 approved clips | Streamers buy on numbers; generic pitches convert near zero. |

## 3. The 12-week plan

### Weeks 1 to 2: set up and get paid once
- One niche (GTA 6 streamers), one account each on YouTube, TikTok and
  Instagram. No extra accounts.
- Campaigns: only those with a floor of about 2,000 views or less, more
  than half the budget left, and rules written on the campaign page.
- Post 1 to 3 clips a day per platform. Same clip on all three is fine and
  each platform pays separately.
- **Milestone:** 10 approved clips and one completed withdrawal. Compare
  views × rate against the balance to learn the real take-home rate.

### Weeks 3 to 6: prove reach
- Keep what works, drop any campaign that hasn't reviewed clips in a week.
- **Move on when:** about 30 approved clips, median above 5,000 views per
  clip, and 2 or 3 clips above 50,000.
- Then apply to application-only campaigns and those with 10,000-view
  floors (higher caps, less competition).
- **Stop rule:** if the median is still under 1,500 views at week 6, change
  the niche or format. Do not add accounts.

### Weeks 7 to 9: direct outreach
- Pick 10 to 15 streamers with roughly 1,000 or more live viewers.
- Clip them publicly first. Then send one clip from last night's stream,
  your view numbers, and a priced paid trial.
- **Move on when:** one paid trial.

### Weeks 10 to 12: retainer
- Written terms: volume, turnaround, revisions, payment timing, whose
  channel hosts the clips, footage permission.
- A realistic first deal is $300 to $800 a month (vendor ranges; press
  reports $500 to $1,500 for proven clippers).

## 6. How posting works, and why not browser automation

- **What the terms say (official, read from archived copies):** YouTube
  bars access by "automated means" without written permission. TikTok
  lists automated scripts that interact with the service. Instagram bars
  automated account creation and data collection; posting is a grey area.
- **Consequence:** Claude driving the upload pages in your browser risks a
  ban, and a ban forfeits unpaid views. This plan does not do it.
- **Official upload APIs (official):** a home-built uploader posts private
  only until the app passes each platform's audit, so that route is closed
  for now too.
- **Compliant routes:** a scheduling app that has already passed those
  audits, or posting by hand from the phone app.
- **Division of work:** Claude makes the clips, writes hooks, titles and
  descriptions, picks the thumbnail frame time, and keeps the sheet. The
  scheduler or you press publish. You, or Claude with your go-ahead, submit
  each link in Content Rewards within 30 minutes.
- **Known limits:** picking a Shorts thumbnail frame is done in the mobile
  app. Desktop Reels reportedly lack trending audio.

## 7. Content rules for every clip

- **Length:** 15 to 45 seconds, ending on the payoff. Never over 60 on
  Shorts: a claimed Short over a minute is blocked (official).
- **Open on the reaction**, then show how it happened. Hook text on screen
  from the first frame, 4 to 8 words.
- **Add something:** hook, context line ("3 hours in, no deaths, then
  this"), a tighter cut. Subtitles alone do not count as original on
  Instagram (press, quoting Instagram).
- **Disclosure at the start of the video.** Content Rewards' terms make a
  missing one a valid rejection. Also switch on each platform's paid
  promotion setting (YouTube "paid promotion", TikTok "content
  disclosure", Instagram "Paid partnership"). Instagram and YouTube say
  the label does not reduce reach; TikTok is disputed.
- **Brief compliance:** streamer is the main focus, name and required
  hashtag in the caption, no negative framing, overlay visible where
  required.
- **Clean files only.** No other app's watermark; Instagram downranks
  them.
- **One edit per campaign.** Reusing the same edit in two campaigns gets
  rejected.
- **Finding moments:** loud spikes (the clipper script), chat-speed
  spikes, and the streamer's most-viewed viewer clips of the last 24
  hours. Being early matters: clips posted within minutes of a moment
  beat polished ones posted a day later (vendor case study).

Hook patterns that practitioners report working:
- Reaction first: "he did NOT expect that"
- Question: "why is he eating out of a vase"
- Stakes: "1 HP left, 400k on the line"
- Insider vocabulary of the streamer's own community in the caption

## 8. What top clippers earn and how

**Documented (press):**
- N3on paid $1.4 million to 303 clippers in five weeks at $0.40 to $0.50
  per 1,000 views. That averages about $4,600 each. Volume beats rate, and
  this program is not open to new accounts.
- One campaign (ForgeGUI) spread $184,425 across 3,632 creators, about
  $51 each; its top clipper made about $20,000.
- Retainers of $500 to $1,500 a month for 20 to 30 posts.
- A clipping business making $20,000 to $30,000 a month.

**Self-claimed, no proof shown:**
- £10,000 a month after 12 to 18 months (BBC interview).
- About $4,000 a month clipping for influencers (NPR interview).
- "62,000 clippers averaging $3,000 a month" from an agency founder. This
  does not survive arithmetic against the company's reported sales.

**The distribution:**
- Content Rewards reports $18 million paid and 600,000 people who have
  posted, about $30 lifetime per poster. About 80% of signups never post.
- Per campaign: bottom quarter earns $8 or less, top quarter $58 or more,
  top tenth $127 or more (vendor).
- Four organisations account for 52% of campaign spend; a quarter of
  campaigns have spent nothing (vendor).
- "The vast majority make little to nothing" (analyst quoted by the BBC).

**How the top ones do it:**
- They treat campaigns as a ladder to direct deals and retainers.
- They specialise in a streamer or niche and learn its community.
- They post fast and often, and they add editing value.
- The largest run teams or agencies and take a share of client spend.
- Press also reports elite clippers running hundreds of accounts. That
  conflicts with platform rules on bulk and duplicate posting; this plan
  does not copy it.

## 9. Platform rules that shape the plan

- **YouTube (press quoting YouTube, Oct 2026):** reach is reduced for
  channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos
  without significant changes". Campaign permission does not exempt a
  channel: reused-content review is "not based on copyright, permission,
  or fair use" (official).
- **Instagram (press quoting Mosseri, Apr 2026):** accounts that mostly
  repost are not recommended to non-followers. Eligibility returns 30 days
  after the last unoriginal post. Ten or more unoriginal posts in 30 days
  removes an account from recommendations (vendor summary).
- **TikTok (official):** unedited reposts stay up but are ineligible for
  the For You feed.
- **Spam (official):** YouTube lists posting the same content repeatedly
  across channels. TikTok bans automation that operates accounts in bulk.
- **Warm-up periods:** no platform documents one. Using the account
  normally for a few days first is cheap folklore, harmless to follow.
- **Ranking signals:** YouTube reports "viewed vs swiped away"; Instagram
  named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Hook in the first
  two seconds, short enough to finish, worth sending to a friend.
- **Shorts ad revenue from Feb 1, 2027 (official):** existing partners need
  10 million qualified Shorts views per 90 days; new applicants need 1,000
  subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours or 20 million qualified Shorts views.
- **TikTok Creator Rewards (official):** 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in
  30 days, original videos of a minute or more. Closed to clip accounts in
  practice.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | What it means | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Rockstar takedown | Its policy allows only non-commercial footage and it can remove clips at any time. Paid clipping arguably falls outside the permission. | Clip streamer reactions, no spoilers or isolated cutscenes, accept that clips can vanish. |
| Unpaid work | Budgets drain before review; approvals get reversed. | Check budget remaining and campaign chat for complaints before each batch. |
| Reach throttling | Repost-heavy accounts lose recommendations. | Add visible value to every clip; one account per platform. |
| Account ban | Forfeits unpaid views. | No bots, no bought views, no engagement groups, no browser automation, no bought accounts. |
| Slow money | 7-day earning round plus 3-day hold, then 3 to 5 days to withdraw. | First cash arrives about two weeks after the first approval. |
| Tax | This is self-employment income in the US; self-employment tax applies from $400 net a year. | Keep the posting sheet as the income record. Ask your accountant how to report it. |
| Market direction | Rates are flat near $1 and the plain-repost tier is being squeezed. | Build toward direct deals from week 7. |
